# Positioning note (T-G3-12)

Facts about other apps come from the public pages linked at the end; I paraphrased, and anything from reviews or third parties is marked. Their features and prices change; re-check before quoting anything.
Jixxy's own features below are **written in code, not yet seen working on a phone** (see `growth/CLAIMS.md`).

## The one-line difference
Other apps ask you to behave. Jixxy is a character who **reacts**: a sarcastic cat who stops you with a joke, lets you choose Leave or Stay, and is funny enough that you want to keep him around. It is a companion, not a lock and not a score.

## Who we are compared with

| Group | Example | What it does (public sources) | Where Jixxy differs | Where it does **not** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Friction apps** | one sec | Intercepts a chosen app and runs a short exercise, by default a breathing one, before you continue; Android users can change the length and the phrase (one sec's own tutorial). Third-party reviews say the free tier covers one app and extra customisation is paid, and that the pause can turn into a habit after a few weeks. | A character and comedy instead of an exercise; you pick the apps; free with no tiers; he also reacts to moments (charger, late night), not only app opens. | Same basic idea (interrupt before the app). | **Virtual-pet / self-care apps** | Finch | You look after a bird by completing self-care tasks; it grows, earns rewards and explores (Finch's store page and reviews). It sits in the health and fitness category and has a paid tier. | Our cat is not a thing you feed or a score to maintain. ## What is genuinely ours (and what we can say)
1. **Personality as the product.** A sarcastic but loyal cat with a voice, 700+ lines and a writing rulebook. No other app in the table leads with comedy. *Claim only after testers say it is funny.*
2. **A choice, not a wall.** Leave or Stay on every card. *"Written"; the Pause is the riskiest feature and has not been seen on a phone.*
3. **Offline and small.** No internet permission, no accounts, no ads: **proven** by CI guards (`growth/CLAIMS.md` #1 to #6, #22).
4. **You set how naggy he is.** Calm / Naggy / Chaos. *Written, not seen.*
5. **Made for Android and for people who find lectures annoying.** Tone for 18 to 30, English first with an opt-in Hinglish beta.

## What we must not claim
- That Jixxy reduces screen time or fixes habits. We have no evidence; testers will give opinions, not outcomes (`growth/TESTER_PROGRAM.md` section 8).
- Anything medical or about addiction. Finch sits in the health category; Jixxy does not.
- That he works better than, or "instead of", one sec, Digital Wellbeing or any named app. Those apps are in different leagues of proof.
- A guilt angle. The writer's rules (`content/SAFETY_LINES.md`) rule it out, and it is the opposite of the brand.

## Risks to the position
- **Novelty fades.** One third-party review of a friction app says the pause loses power over time. A joke pause could fade the same way. Fresh lines and the nag dial are our defences; the 7-day tester check is the first test.
- **Annoyance.** Naggy by design can tip into annoying. Q1 of the tester form measures it.
- **Review risk on Play.** See `store/DISTRIBUTION_OPTIONS.md` B8.

## Suggested public wording (for README or the store, once the build is verified)
"A lazy, sarcastic cat who sits on your screen and stops you, with a joke, before the apps you choose. Leave or Stay: your call. Free, offline, no ads, no accounts."
Do not add comparisons.
